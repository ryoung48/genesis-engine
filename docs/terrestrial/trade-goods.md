# Trade goods

Every non-desolate land location gets one trade good (`TRADE_GOODS.computeTradeGoods`, stage `Post: trade goods` at the end of `pipelines/post-elevation`). Nothing in the model reads it; it colours the trade goods map and the hover panel.

Code: `src/model/society/infrastructure/trade/trade-goods`; table in `trade-goods-table`, generated by `scripts/gen-trade-goods-table.py`.

## Table

The table counts EU5 locations by `climate|vegetation|topography|coastal` and raw material, read from the EU5toGIS dataset (DavidRMilos): `locations.gpkg` for the labels and raw material, `ports.gpkg` for coastal (the location has a port). `mountain_wasteland` counts as mountains; other wastelands, atolls and water are skipped, as is inland fish in arid and cold-arid land outside wetlands. EU5's farmland vegetation is kept as its own vegetation (766 locations). Before blending, the counts outside farmland match the previous spreadsheet-built table within 1.4%.

### Oil and gas

EU5 has no oil, so oil and gas come from PRIO's PETRODATA v1.2 onshore field polygons (Lujala, Rød & Thieme 2007; kept beside EU5toGIS in `Downloads/PETRODATA`). An EU5 location whose representative point lies in a field (2,501 of 20,744, 12.1%: the polygons are basin-scale) adds 0.0434 of an oil and gas location to its row. That fraction gives oil and gas coal's EU5 world share (0.52%): the deposits layer puts coal and oil and gas on the same share of land (4.7% of land regions). Field coverage, and so the weight, is highest on wetlands (18.5% of locations) and flatland (16.2%) and lowest on mountains (3.7%), in arctic climates (5.5%) and jungle (6.9%). Offshore fields are left out: offshore production starts in 1947. Rows then average 5.6 oil and gas per 1,000 (coal's EU5 share is 5.2).

### Merged goods

Placement cannot tell EU5's regional variants apart, so they are merged into one good each before counting: **grain** is wheat, rice, millet, maize and potato (EU5 12.3% of locations together), and **spices** are pepper, cloves, saffron and chili (1.0%), and **gems** take amber (0.15%, Baltic only). Elephants are dropped; with oil and gas added that leaves 45 goods. Colours are keyed by label (grain takes wheat's, spices saffron's).

### Blending thin rows

139 of the 379 EU5 combinations rest on fewer than 10 locations, so one location can be a third or half of its row (`cold_arid|woods|wetlands|coastal`: 2 locations, one of them fiber crops). Each row is blended with its parents, keeping climate and coast at every level so coastal goods never reach inland rows:

1. the row, `climate|vegetation|topography|coast`;
2. the same climate, topography and coast over all vegetation;
3. the same climate and coast.

A level's own counts `c` weigh against its parent's shares `P` as `(c + k·P) / (n + k)`, `n` its location count, with `k = 10`: at 10 locations a 10% share has a binomial standard error of about 9.5 points, as large as the share, so a row that small takes half its mix from its parent. A 300-location row keeps 97% of its own mix. Pooling vegetation and topography in the parents would carry goods into classes EU5 never puts them in (lumber reached desert rows at 8.7% on average), so a good with no EU5 location in a whole vegetation or topography class of at least 600 locations is kept out of that class's rows: were its share 0.5%, zero in 600 locations would happen about 5% of the time. That keeps amber, cocoa, fur, ivory, lumber, olives, tea and tobacco out of desert; amber, cocoa and lumber out of sparse; cocoa, coffee, lumber, mercury, silver and tobacco out of farmland; coffee, olives and wine out of jungle; tobacco out of plateau; and coffee, marble and mercury out of wetlands. Every combination with a parent gets a row (560), including ones EU5 never had. Weights are the blended shares per 1,000, shares under 0.05% dropped.

On the default 204k worlds (seeds 14963991 and 777, 2026-11-27) blending took fiber crops from 33% and 12.7% of their locations sitting in solid blocks (most of a location's 24 nearest neighbours holding the same good) to 0%, and clay from 8% to 0.4%. No good's world share moved more than 1.5 points (fiber crops −1.5 and −0.8, lumber +1.0 and +0.7).

## Location class

A location's climate, vegetation and topography are the most common combination over its land regions (climate from Pasta, as before; on a tie, the first combination to reach the top count). It is coastal when any of its regions is coastal, like an EU5 location with a port.

## Farmland

Farmland locations draw from the farmland rows. Candidates are locations whose province's people mostly farm at the start year (`wayOfLife.farm ≥ 0.5`, the majority capitals need). Their score is farmer density, `farm × density`, times EU5's farmland share for the location's topography. Per climate, the best-scoring candidates become farmland until the climate holds EU5's farmland share:

In EU5, farmland clusters in river floodplains of old dense farming (Nile delta, Yangtze delta, Flanders, the Ganges doab, Paris basin, Punjab): 30% of a farmland location's six nearest neighbours are farmland, against 3.6% of all locations. Worlds without a farming clock (Earth imports, the distribution history pipeline) get no farmland.

## Livelihood gates

Before the draw, each table weight is multiplied by a gate read from the province's way of life at the start year (`FARMING_WAY_OF_LIFE.wayOfLife`):

- **Crop goods** (grain, legumes, cotton, sugar, tobacco, cocoa, coffee, tea, spices, olives, wine, fruit, fiber crops, silk): times the farm share. A crop good means people grow it.
- **Animal goods** (livestock, wool, horses): zero unless herds have arrived (`farming.herdAdoptionYear` at or before the start year), otherwise times farm plus herd share. Farmers who keep cattle (mixed farming) keep animal goods; farmers whom herds never reached do not. In EU5, North American farmers grow maize, beans and squash (crops 22.6% of North American locations against 28% in Europe) but livestock, wool and horses are 0.9% there against 21.7% in Europe.
- **Wild goods** (wild game, ivory, fur, lumber): times `forage · b + r · (1 − forage)`, with `forage = 1 − farm − herd`. `r` is EU5's share of the good in farmed Europe over its share in mostly foraging North America, continental climate only (2,090 and 908 locations): wild game 5.5% / 17.2% = 0.32, fur 4.0% / 16.0% = 0.25, lumber 9.6% / 23.2% = 0.41; ivory takes wild game's. North America's continental zone includes eastern farmers, so these ratios understate the contrast a little. The table's rows average over EU5's forager and farmed land, so forager land gets the weight that average implies there, `b = 1 / (φ + (1 − φ) r)`, with `φ` the share of the climate's EU5 locations in forager land in 1337 (North America north of 24° N, Siberia, Australia, the Amazon and South America south of 35° S; 4,105 of 20,846 locations): arctic 0.657, cold arid 0.201, continental 0.192, subtropical 0.160, arid 0.113, oceanic 0.107, tropical 0.086, mediterranean 0.057. Farmed land keeps the full `r` cut, so rows no longer average to EU5's shares; continental wild game gets ×2.3 in forager land and ×0.32 in farmed land. Medicaments (1.9% / 2.5%) and beeswax (higher in Europe) are not gated.
- **Everything else** (minerals, fish, dyes, incense, medicaments, beeswax): ungated. Elephants are not a good: their EU5 locations are left out of the counts.

If the gates zero a location's whole row, it draws from the ungated row. Worlds without a farming clock get no gates.

## Regional goods

Tobacco, cocoa, tea and coffee are held to one home landmass each (`REGIONAL_GOODS.confine`, `trade-goods/regional`). In EU5 they sit in one region far beyond what climate explains: against the climate table alone at the same share, EU5's same-good concentration at 500 to 1,500 and 1,500 to 4,000 km is 4.6× and 6.9× for tobacco and 3.8× and 2.5× for cocoa (tea 1.8× and 0.9×; coffee has 15 EU5 locations).

Confinement ends with the Columbian Exchange: from leader TL 4.09 (`LIVESTOCK_VOYAGES.openOceanLevel`, the level at which ships carry livestock across the open ocean, Columbus's second voyage in 1493) the four goods keep their first, unconfined draw everywhere their climate and livelihood gates allow, as tobacco, cocoa and coffee spread to plantations on every suitable continent after 1500 (Crosby 1972, *The Columbian Exchange*).

1. A first draw runs as usual. Each good's home is the landmass of the lowest-index location that drew it.
2. If home holds under half (`HOME_MIN_SUITABLE_SHARE = 0.5`) of the good's suitable weight world-wide (its expected count over all locations), the nearest other landmass that can grow it is added, by coast-to-coast distance (200 sampled coastal locations per landmass), until the set holds half. Landmass pairs whose bounding spheres are already farther apart than the best gap so far are skipped, and pair gaps are shared across the four goods.
3. The good's weight is zero off the home set and scaled up on it (eight fixed-point rounds) so its expected world count equals the unconfined one.
4. Only locations whose options include a confined good are redrawn, with the same per-location seed.

On the default worlds (2026-11-27) tobacco, cocoa and coffee ended on 1 to 3 landmasses and tea on 3. Counts against the same worlds unconfined: seed 14963991 tobacco 20 → 21, cocoa 15 → 15, tea 68 → 69, coffee 2 → 2 (the crop gate had already put them on the one farming continent; 105 locations changed); seed 777 tobacco 35 → 33, cocoa 86 → 72, tea 98 → 92, coffee 13 → 13. Spill can add a one-location island when it is the nearest landmass that can grow the good. Stage time (median of 15 calls on the same inputs, 2026-11-27) is 118 and 130 ms on the two worlds, about the 0.12 s it took before the livelihood gates. The ~855,000 gated options sit in flat typed arrays (offsets per location) and confinement rescales them in place. Location classes pack each region's class into one number (climate · 64 + biome · 8 + topography) to count them. The largest steps left are confinement (~30 ms), the first draw and the gates (~20 ms each).

## Tech-level gates

Some goods only appear once the world's leading provinces reach a tech level (`GOOD_MIN_TECH_LEVEL`, compared with `TECH_LEVEL.leaderLevel` of the world's average, 3.15 at the default target). Gated goods are taken out of the table rows before anything else, so a row left empty falls back to the next candidate row as a missing row does.

| Good | Lowest leader TL | Why |
|---|---|---|
| copper, tin | 1 | copper smelting and bronze (Copper Age) |
| iron | 2 | Iron Age |
| coal | 2 | burnt as fuel in Han China |
| saltpeter | 3 | gunpowder, Tang China (Early Middle Ages) |
| oil and gas | 5 | first commercial wells, 1850s (Industrial Revolution) |

Ages are the tech-level scale's descriptors (`society/tech-level`). Measured on seed 14963991 (2026-11-27): at the default target, no oil and gas and coal 0.85% of locations; at target 6, oil and gas 0.76% and coal 0.85%; at target 0.8, none of the six gated goods.

## Draw

Each location draws one good from its gated row with a generator seeded per location (`RNG.weightedIndex`, the same pick `weightedChoice` uses, over the flat weights).
