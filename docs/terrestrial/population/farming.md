# Farming arrival and way of life

A deterministic, generation-time field that gives each habitable province a farming arrival year (or never), the hearth the package came from, and a way of life at any year: forage, farm or herd. It is computed in `pipelines/post-elevation` right after migration (`FARMING.compute`), stored as `world.farming`, and read by two Population map modes, the detailed history report and an Earth diagnostic. Its way-of-life field (`FARMING_WAY_OF_LIFE.wayOfLife`) also drives the planet's start-year clock (`TECH_START`), the start-year population density weights and the farm-and-herd share that steers state and capital placement (both in `post-elevation`), the no-towns-in-foraging-land rule in urbanization (`generate-world`), and the way-of-life TL caps (`tech-level/spread`). It draws no random numbers and never changes the migration arrival field: farming waits for foragers and never settles land itself. Cradles, founding and the migration speed-up remain deferred to [the capacity plan](../../../plans/farming-arrival-and-capacity.md).

The hearth-odds factors, spread rules and capacities are adapted from upstream PR #88 of `raguilar011095/planet_heightmap_generation` at commit `632f0ec675d1d61b6d131514a923e25b59601840` (GPL-3.0). Only the documented numerical rules are reimplemented in this repository's own structures; upstream's ignition machinery, pixel pipeline and UI are not ported. Not ported: wild-crop niches, crop variety and the `tame` gate (seasonality leads the odds instead), per-crop searches, beasts, the tsetse belt, founder windows, exponential ignition waits and contact notices.

No input is latitude, hemisphere, longitude, a named Earth region or an Earth-only quantity: every term reads the planet's own climate, terrain, water and province graph.

## Climate measures

Per province, area-weighted over its land regions (`FARMING_MEASURES`), from the monthly temperature (°C), rainfall (mm) and PET fields, elevation, vegetation, topography, rivers and Pasta's growing degree days:

- `T`, annual mean temperature; `P`, annual rain; `range`, warmest minus coldest month; `Tc`, the coldest month's mean temperature.
- `arid` = P ÷ annual PET (the UNEP aridity index); zero PET gives 0 for zero rain and infinity otherwise.
- `seas`, Walsh & Lawler (1981) rain seasonality, `sum(|rain[m] − P/12|) / P`, 0 when P is 0.
- `relief`, the range of mean elevation over the province and its same-landmass land neighbours (km).
- `npp`, the Miami model (Lieth 1975), `min(3000 / (1 + e^(1.315 − 0.119T)), 3000 (1 − e^(−0.000664P)))` g/m²/yr.
- `forager`, density in people/km², `0.01 + 0.49 · S([250, 1500], npp)`, doubled (`AQUATIC = 2`) on an ocean coast or with a visible river. Marlowe (2005) tabulates 312 forager societies with median 0.12, mean 0.30 and range under 0.01 to 11 people/km²; the doubling takes the conservative low end of upstream's 1–3 on the richest coasts (2 × 0.5 = 1).
- `gdd`, Pasta's growing degree days, reused as is. A frost-free year gives `Infinity` (all-year growing), which saturates every growing-season term; a province mixing such regions with finite ones is also `Infinity`.
- `pasture`, grassland area, plus half of sparse-vegetation area and a quarter of woods area (wood pasture, dehesa, savanna woodland; closed forest and jungle count zero), over land area. `floodShare`, the area share of land regions with both a visible river and the flatland class, a binary proxy for upstream's mapped floodplain.

`S([a, b], x)` is `MATH.smoothstep`, including reversed edges. The growing-season factor is `S([350, 1300], gdd)`, Pasta's own tundra and boreal-to-temperate boundaries; because Pasta caps GDD by insolation, a dim star or long nights shorten the season even where it is warm.

## Hearth odds

Seasonality leads: a sharp dry or cold season favoured annual seed plants and forced storage through a lean season. Matranga (2024) finds one standard deviation more temperature seasonality (about 8 °C) goes with adopting agriculture about 1,000 years earlier. `FARMING_ODDS` multiplies, for traversable provinces (non-desolate and settled by 2025):

`odds = seasF · S([250, 900], npp) · hillF · water · richF · season · cold`

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| `seasF` | `S([0.5, 1], seasonRank)`; the rank is the mean of the province's rank in `range` and in `seas` among habitable provinces (zero-based ascending, mean rank for ties, 1 for a lone province). Upstream keeps the higher of the two; on Earth that lets monsoon savannas, whose rain seasonality index exceeds 1, outrank the Fertile Crescent and North China, and the Pinhasi median error was 3,258 years late. The mean puts the Fertile Crescent hearth at −9017 instead of −7017 and halves that error (diagnostic of 2026-11-16). |
| `hillF` | `0.5 + 0.5 · S([0.1, 0.6], relief) · S([4, 9], T)` |
| `water` | `0.55 + 0.45 ·` (river 1, lake 0.6, ocean 0.6, none 0) |
| `richF` | `1 − 0.6 · S([0.8, 2], d)`, `d` the area-weighted fourth-power mean of `forager` over provinces within `RICH_KM = 150` (land-graph walk) |
| `season` | `S([1300, 1800], gdd)`. Hearths need a temperate growing season: 1,300 GDD is Pasta's split between its boreal (`CE`) and temperate (`CD`) continental zones, and upstream allowed no hearths poleward of 44–52°. The 500-GDD ramp is a modeling choice. Revised 2026-11-16 from `S([350, 1300])`, which gave boreal Siberia (1,225 GDD) a hearth on Earth. |
| `cold` | `S([−10, −4], Tc)`, `Tc` the coldest month's mean temperature (°C). Calibrated, not derived (Earth diagnostic, observed climate, 2026-11-16): without it a hearth sat on the Great Plains (46° N, coldest month −10 °C) and seeded North American farming 4,800 years before any real centre there, while the real centres' coldest months are +5 (Fertile Crescent), +4 (Andes), +18 (Mexico), +1 (Ohio) and −4 (Yellow River). Edges [−10, −4] gave ArchaeoGLOBE sum 4.042 against 4.006 with Pinhasi unchanged; [−12, −6] gave the same 4.042; [−8, −2] gave 3.999 and moved the Pinhasi median to −656. The Plains hearth is replaced by a weaker California one (39° N, −122°, −4631), so North America is still seeded too early. |

Odds are normalised by the maximum eligible raw odds; with none positive there are no hearths.

**Known gap: vegeculture.** Root and tree-crop centres such as the New Guinea highlands sit in wet tropics with weak seasons and are expected to be missed. If no fired hearth lands within 700 km of that centre, a follow-up plan adds one vegeculture term (frost-free, wet, productive land).

**Where versus when (2026-11-17).** Hearth odds and hearth timing use different seasonality. For *where*, cool tropical climates are judged on rain seasonality alone: with `tropical = S([10, 5], range) · S([26, 22], T)` (a small annual range and a mean below about 24 °C, i.e. tropical highlands), the seasonality rank is `(1 − tropical) · mean(temperature rank, rain rank) + tropical · rain rank`. The mean-of-ranks rule alone gave the Andes and the Mexican highlands zero odds. For *when*, the start-year map reads a separate timing score with the plain mean-of-ranks seasonality (temperature seasonality predicts earlier farming, Matranga 2024), times the same site factors. Earth diagnostic, observed climate: ArchaeoGLOBE sum 4.13 → 4.26, Pinhasi unchanged (Spearman 0.748, median −107, median absolute 579). Using the tropical rule for timing as well let East African and Angolan highlands outrank the Fertile Crescent (its hearth fell to −7518, Pinhasi median +1,068) and gave 3.85; partial tropical credit in timing traded way-of-life agreement for earlier American hearths (credit 0.25: 4.21, centre timing error 3,109 years; 0.5: 4.04, 2,752; 0: 4.26, 3,364). Credit 0 was chosen for way-of-life agreement. **Start date versus dependence (2026-11-17).** With the dependence lag in place, the start-date score takes half the cool-tropics rain credit, `START_TROPICAL = 0.5`: `(1 − 0.5 · tropical) · mean rank + 0.5 · tropical · rain rank`. The dependence lag reads a third score with the plain mean-of-ranks seasonality, so earlier tropical starts do not also shorten the lag. Earth diagnostic, observed climate, with the lag at 4,250 years: credit 0 gives ArchaeoGLOBE 4.36, Pinhasi 0.748 / −107 / 579, centre timing error 3,364 years, HYDE density distance 1.09; credit 0.5 gives 4.42, 0.778 / −107 / 575, 2,752 years, 0.99; credit 1 gives 4.29 with the Pinhasi median at +1,068. Balsas moves from 5,600 to 2,000 years late. Calibrated value. The new hearth set adds late false hearths in Tanzania, Angola, Cameroon, north-east Brazil and coastal Ecuador; New Guinea is still missed.

## Hearth schedule and firing

A deterministic schedule replaces upstream's founders and waits (`FARMING_HEARTHS`):

- **Candidates:** eligible provinces with normalised odds at least `REGION_ODDS = 0.15` that are a local maximum within `REGION_KM = 300` (lower id wins ties).
- **Selection:** by descending odds then id; skip a candidate closer than `HEARTH_SPACING = 0.6 · √(farmable km² / 8)` to an accepted hearth (farmable: traversable, `migration.suitability ≥ 0.08`); stop at `MAX_HEARTHS = 12`.
- **Start year:** `FARMING_START_YEAR + FOUNDER_DELAY + (HEARTH_LAST_YEAR − FARMING_START_YEAR − FOUNDER_DELAY) · (1 − odds)`, then the later of that and the province's continuous migration arrival. The odds in this map are rescaled so the best site already settled at `FARMING_START_YEAR` counts as 1 (`min(1, odds / settledBest)`; the raw global maximum if nothing is settled then). Without this, a planet whose best farmland is settled late dates every early-settled hearth by its odds against that unreachable site: on the 204k procedural verification world (seed 14963991), where only 34% of habitable land is settled between 40,000 and 4,000 BCE, the origin landmass's best hearth (odds 0.62) started at −6743 and only 6% of land farmed by −4000. With the rescaling it starts at −9600 and 15% is farmed by −4000 (2026-11-17). Earth is unchanged, since its best sites are settled long before −9700.

`FARMING_START_YEAR = −9700` is the base of the Holocene on the simulation's shared calendar (Walker et al. 2009, 11,700 b2k); Larson et al. (2014) name 12,000–9,000 BP as the first main period of domestication. `FOUNDER_DELAY = 100` is upstream's lower founder bound, so the best site starts in −9600. `HEARTH_LAST_YEAR = −2050` (4,000 BP) ends Larson et al.'s second period (7,000–4,000 BP). The map is linear in normalised odds, which keeps upstream's ordering (better sites earlier) without a random stream.

A hearth fires only if its site holds no farming label at or before its start year (decided in the spread search).

## Spread search

One earliest-arrival search with one label per province (`FARMING_SPREAD`): continuous arrival, `hearthOf` (source hearth province), `farmingFrom` (predecessor, −1 at a hearth). Equal keys are processed by province id then hearth province id. Only strictly earlier arrivals relax, so the first label at a junction wins; this trades upstream's per-hearth labels for one array per field.

**Settlement wait.** Traversal covers non-desolate provinces with a migration arrival. Every edge arrival is clamped to the destination's continuous migration arrival (farmers wait for foragers) and the wait delays that province's outgoing edges. The search stops at 2025.

**Land edges** use migration's same-landmass `landGraph` (non-desolate provinces only: foragers walk across desolate land in the migration search, but farming never does, and a farming front still cannot cross it). Into a farm or forage destination the speed is `BASE_SPEED · fit · like · rich · channel`, where `like` judges each step of a climate gradient on its own rather than against the distant hearth:

| Factor | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `BASE_SPEED` | | 0.35 km/yr, calibrated on the Earth diagnostic with observed climate (2026-11-16) against Pinhasi, Fort & Ammerman's 765 dated sites, after `like` was changed to the local likeness. Sweep of the worktree-comparable block (Spearman, signed median, median absolute error, ArchaeoGLOBE sum): 0.3 gives 0.776, +563, 829, 3.44; **0.35 gives 0.758, −99, 580, 3.35**; 0.4 gives 0.739, −555, 732, 3.20; 0.5 gives 0.719, −1,222, 1,270, 3.10; 0.6 gives 0.707, −1,655, 1,685, 3.01. 0.35 is where the median crosses zero and the median absolute error is lowest, and it keeps Spearman above the earlier 0.751. Judging each step against the province the front leaves rather than the distant hearth removes a penalty the old likeness applied cumulatively, so the earlier 0.6 now runs far too fast. Final modelled front slope 0.88 km/yr against the observed 0.99 (published 0.6-1.3). |
| `fit` | `0.03 + 0.97 · S([0.08, 0.45], suitability)` | upstream `fitSpeed`, 3% floor |
| `like` | `0.05 + 0.95 · e^(−(ΔT/6)² − (ln(P/P₀)/1.6)²)` between the destination and the province the front is leaving (the edge's source), not the source hearth | `LIKE_T = 6`, `LIKE_W = 1.6`, `LIKE_FLOOR = 0.05` |
| `rich` | `√(1 − 0.8 · S([0.25, 1.4], forager))` | upstream `richSlow` |
| `channel` | 4 with a visible river, 3 on an ocean coast, else 1 | upstream `river`, `riverCoast` |

Into a herd destination the speed is `HERD_SPEED · herdFit · rich` with `HERD_SPEED = 3` km/yr and `herdFit = 0.03 + 0.97 · S([0.3, 0.8], pasture)`; no `like` or `channel`, since herds follow pasture rather than source-adapted crops and steppe herders needed no river corridors. The southern African foraging-to-herding transition spread at about 2.4 km/yr (PLoS ONE 2014), twice the European Neolithic, and 3 km/yr gives 2.4 at `herdFit = 0.8`.

**Sea edges** reuse migration's indexed open-water links between landmasses and `MIGRATION.transit` with kind `"sea"` (capability knots, target reach, landing delay), with no length cap, since farmers carried the Lapita and Polynesian settlement. Shelf routes are excluded: the window opens after the shelf has closed.

The winning continuous time rounds up to a whole `farmingYear`, with `MIGRATION.sentinel` for unreached provinces or arrivals after 2025.

## Ways of life

`FARMING_WAY_OF_LIFE.modeOf({ field, province })` reads the way-of-life field and returns `"uninhabited"` before the migration arrival (and for desolate or never-settled provinces), `"forage"` from settlement until the adoption year (always, when it never adopts), then the post-farming mode. Three capacities in people/km² choose the mode (`FARMING_LIVING`):

| Capacity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Forage | `forager` density |
| Farm | `60 · max(rainfed, irrigated)`; rainfed = `suitability · S([0.2, 0.5], arid) · S([350, 1300], gdd)`; irrigated = `2.5 · floodShare` in any climate (floodplains and wet-rice plains as well as irrigated desert valleys) |
| Herd | `2 · pasture · S([350, 1300], gdd)`: no herding on tundra (below Pasta's 350-GDD tundra boundary) |

`FARM_PEOPLE_PER_KM2 = 60` is upstream `peoplePerFood`; the 1328 hearth survey (Lot 1929) implies 30–40 people/km² over present-day France and about 60 or more around Paris, so land of suitability 0.5–0.7 gives 30–42. The aridity bounds are the UNEP semi-arid class, where rain-fed cropping is possible but unreliable (Middleton & Thomas 1997). `IRRIGATION_YIELD = 2.5` is a conservative reading of Butzer (1976): about 180 people/km² overall in Egypt, three times the rain-fed 60. `HERD_PEOPLE_PER_KM2 = 2` is upstream `peoplePerPasture`.

Post-farming mode: a mode must first cover at least `MODE_SHARE_MIN = 0.27` of the province's land, then beat forage on capacity. Farm share is `cropFit = S([1000, 2000], gdd) · max(S([250, 900], npp), floodShare)`: growing season times moisture, where moisture is net primary productivity or flood-plain share, whichever is larger. Herd share is `(1 − cropFit) · pasture · S([350, 1300], gdd)`, the grazing share of the land the crops don't take. Farm is allowed when `cropFit ≥ 0.27` and farm capacity exceeds forage; herd when its share is at least 0.27 and herd capacity exceeds forage. If both are allowed, farm if farm ≥ herd, else herd; if neither, the province forages for good. The reliability term is the difference from upstream: without it farm wins whenever `60 · suitability > 2 · pasture` and semi-arid steppe comes out as farmland. The farm share uses its own season ramp, `FARM_SEASON = S([1000, 2000], gdd)`, stricter than the survival ramp `S([350, 1300])`: farming becomes the dominant way of life only where the season is long enough for reliable cereal yields, while marginal seasons stay mixed with foraging and herding. On Earth the regions wrongly labelled farm sat at 800–1,530 GDD (Canada, Volga, Central Russia, Belarus, Scandinavia, Siberia) against 2,800–6,000 GDD for correctly farmed cores. The edges are calibrated on the Earth diagnostic (observed climate, 2026-11-16): ArchaeoGLOBE sum 3.68 with no farm ramp; [800, 1800] 3.99; [1000, 2000] 4.01; [1200, 2200] 4.02; [1300, 2500] 3.95; [1300, 3000] 3.97, a plateau, with Pinhasi unchanged (Spearman 0.748, median −107, median absolute error 579). Choosing farm versus herd by land share instead of capacity gave 3.99 and was not kept.

| Land | Inputs | Farm | Herd | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold semi-arid steppe | suitability 0.1, arid 0.25, npp 150, pasture 0.8 | 0.44 | 1.6 | herd |
| Semi-arid millet belt | suitability 0.3, arid 0.35, npp 600, pasture 0.5 | 9 | 1.0 | farm |
| Desert river valley | suitability 0.05, arid 0.05, npp 100, floodShare 0.3, pasture 0.1 | 45 | 0.2 | farm |
| Desert interior | suitability 0.03, arid 0.05, npp 100, floodShare 0, pasture 0.3, forage 0.01 | 0 | 0.6 | herd |
| Rich forager coast | suitability 0.4, arid 1.2, npp 1000, forage 1.0 | 24 | — | farm at arrival |

**Herd animals.** Herding needs domesticable herd animals, and those survived only where large herbivores coevolved with hominins. Late Quaternary megafauna extinction was least severe where hominins evolved and most severe on landmasses that modern humans reached as the first hominins (Sandom et al. 2014, *Proc. R. Soc. B* 281: 20133254). The rule: after the spread, a province may herd only if its farming came from a hearth on the migration origin's landmass; otherwise it is re-decided with no herd share, so it farms if it can and forages if not. The herd package travels with farming, so islands farmed from the origin landmass can still herd. On Earth this removes herding from the Americas and Australia (the Andean llama is the known exception the rule misses) until herds sail there by [livestock voyages](#herding-beyond-the-farm-frontier). Earth diagnostic, observed climate (2026-11-17): ArchaeoGLOBE sum 4.04 → 4.13, Pinhasi unchanged (Spearman 0.748, median −107, median absolute 579), HYDE density distance 0.915 → 0.923. Adding a grassland sod penalty, `cropFit · (1 − k · grassland share)` for prairie sod that resisted cultivation until heavy and steel ploughs (Kendall, *John Deere's Steel Plow*, Smithsonian), lowered the sum (k = 0.5: 4.06; k = 0.8: 4.07) and was not kept.

**Adoption.** Farming or herding is first adopted `ADOPTION_LAG_YEARS = round(RAMP_YEARS · ln 2) = 451` years after the package arrives. `RAMP_YEARS = 650` is the `farming-arrival-capacity` worktree's settlement ramp, a modelling choice within the 500–700-year demographic-transition signal (Bocquet-Appel 2006); the half-way point of that ramp is the earliest a province counts as having left forage. On top of the lag, foragers hold while capacity falls short of `hold(t) = 1 + (HOLD − 1) · e^(−t / HOLD_DECAY_YEARS)`, with `t` years since arrival. With `r = c / forage`: `r ≥ HOLD` adopts at `farmingYear + 451`; `1 < r < HOLD` adopts at `farmingYear + 451 + HOLD_DECAY_YEARS · ln((HOLD − 1)/(r − 1))` rounded up; an adoption year past 2025 is never; `r ≤ 1` never. `HOLD = 10` is upstream `hold`. `HOLD_DECAY_YEARS = 1500` follows the stable forager–farmer frontier of about 1,500 years between Linearbandkeramik farmers (northern Germany and the Netherlands around 5500 cal BC) and the Ertebølle and Swifterbant lands (farming around 4000 cal BC; Rowley-Conwy). So `r = 5` adopts about 1,650 years after arrival, `r = 2` about 3,750, and `r = 9` about 630. The spreading front does not wait for adoption: the package passes through holdout provinces at their slowed speed.

**Herding beyond the farm frontier.** Pastoralism also spread without crops. A second front starts from the fired hearths on the migration origin's landmass at their start years and runs the same spread search with every province treated as herd land (`HERD_SPEED · herdFit · rich`). A province reached by it adopts herding when its herd capacity beats forager density (no 0.27 land-share gate: desert and sparse pasture counts), no earlier than the pastoral gate, after the usual adoption lag and hold. `modeOf` returns herd from that year until farming is adopted; density ramps from it.

**Pastoral gate.** The gate is `PASTORAL_DELAY_YEARS = 8600` years after the planet's first fired origin-landmass hearth starts (`FARMING_LIVING.pastoralYear`), so it moves with the planet's own farming timeline instead of sitting at a calendar year. On Earth the first such hearth starts at −9600 (China), so the gate falls at −1000 exactly, the value calibrated before. It stands for specialised and mounted pastoralism, which took off in the early first millennium BCE: the Arzhan-1 kurgan dates to the 9th–8th century BC and south-central Siberia shows a rise in nomadic population density around 850 BC ([PMC3179056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3179056); [Britannica, the Steppe](https://www.britannica.com/place/the-Steppe/Emergence-of-the-pastoral-way-of-life)). Earth diagnostic, observed climate (2026-11-18): ArchaeoGLOBE sum 4.42 without the front; 4.38 ungated (herding by about −6000 lowered the middle slices); gate −4000 4.42, −2000 4.42, −1000 4.44, with the 950 and 1500 slices up from 0.48 / 0.56 to 0.49 / 0.57 and Pinhasi unchanged. The −1000 value is calibrated.

**Livestock voyages.** Herds also cross sea gaps between landmasses, and the herd front above sails them. Every sea link between landmasses is a crossing whose arrival year is the later of the year the front reaches its shore and the year the planet can carry livestock that far, `LIVESTOCK_VOYAGES.crossingYear`, so the shortest gap on the shortest chain lands the herd first. **Relay rule:** a province can send herds across the sea only if it itself adopts herding (its herd adoption year is finite: the pastoral herding test, herd capacity beating forager density, or herds that came with its crops), and only from that adoption year on, so the crossing year is the latest of the front's arrival, the source's adoption and the curve. Provinces the front merely passes through without adopting carry nothing onward by sea (land spread is unchanged, so the Old World front still crosses forest); the landing coastal provinces seed the pastoral front on the new landmass (normal herd speed, adoption lag, hold, gate and capacity test), and a landmass that has herds passes them on. An earlier herd date is never replaced, and a landmass that already herds by farming keeps that date. **Mixed farming.** Livestock that reach a province that already farms (from any hearth, with crop fit) join the farming with no domestication lag: its herd adoption year is the later of the herd front's arrival and its farming adoption, plus `MIXED_HERD_DELAY_YEARS = 30` (`FARMING_LIVING.mixedHerdAdoption`). Colonial cattle and sheep spread within decades of landing: Hispaniola 1493, Mexico in the 1520s (Crosby 1972). Such a province counts as adopting for the relay rule, and its herd land and `postMode` are decided with its herd share. Land with no farming keeps the pure pastoral test and the 451-year adoption lag. Provinces on a landmass reached this way get herd land (`herdFit`) from their own herd adoption year, not from their farming adoption; only land farmed from an origin-landmass hearth gets herds with its crops.

The longest crossing is a curve of target tech level (the world's leading level), log-interpolated in distance between three knots and flat beyond the last:

| Knot | Gap | Target level | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cyprus, about 8500 BCE | 80 km | 0.69 | sheep, goats, cattle and pigs reach Cyprus from the Levant (Vigne et al. 2012, *PNAS* 109: 8445; Zeder 2008, *PNAS* 105: 11597) |
| Madagascar zebu, about 1000 CE | 400 km | 3.22 | zebu cattle cross the Mozambique Channel (Blench 2007, *Azania* 42: 69; Boivin et al. 2013, *J. World Prehistory* 26: 213) |
| Atlantic, 1493 | 1,000 km (`OPEN_OCEAN_KM`) | 4.09 | Columbus's second voyage (Crosby 1972, *The Columbian Exchange*) |

**Two regimes.** Coastal and island-hopping gaps up to `OPEN_OCEAN_KM = 1,000` km grow gradually with distance along the curve. Every longer gap opens at the same level, 4.09 (the Atlantic knot), whatever its length: crossing open ocean arrived as one capability step (ships that carry stock, rigging for open water, navigation out and a way back), not a little later for each extra kilometre. The 400 to 1,000 km stretch interpolates up to that step rather than extending the 80 to 400 km log rate, which would reach level 4.66 at 1,000 km, later than the step itself, so a longer gap would open before a shorter one. Direct routes keep a separate `MAX_ROUTE_KM = 5,000` cap (the Atlantic evidence); Pacific-scale gaps, travelled by livestock only after the 1560s galleon lines, are not modelled.

Each level is the Earth year of the crossing pushed through the calibration pairing of Earth years with average level (`docs/terrestrial/population/tech-levels.md`) and `TECH_LEVEL.leaderLevel`: −8500 → average 0.107 → leader 0.69; 1000 → 2.4 → 3.22; 1493 → 2.99 → 4.09. No Earth year is stored in the model. The planet year of each knot is `TECH_START.targetYear` (the start-year search at the average level of the target), read from the planet's pre-voyage farming timeline (crossings cannot move their own clock), and the year of any other gap is interpolated between those three in log distance. Gaps shorter than 80 km take the first year; gaps beyond 5,000 km take the last. On Earth the knots fall at −8755, 943 and 1494. A crossing that lands after the planet's start year has no effect on the generated snapshot, and nothing spreads during a history run.

**Direct ocean routes.** Adjacent-shore sea links alone gave no way to sail livestock straight to a far coast, so North America and the Caribbean were reached only by island chains that died (Greenland does not adopt herding; the Alaskan front crosses tundra at floor speed). The herd search therefore also gets direct routes (`LIVESTOCK_VOYAGES.routes`): from every coastal province (`waterAccess = 2`), one route to the nearest coastal province of every other landmass within 5,000 km (the last knot). A route opens at the same time as any sea link: the latest of the front's arrival at the source, the source's herd adoption (relay rule unchanged, including herds with crops and mixed farming) and the curve year for the gap. The line is not checked to stay over ocean: the nearest coast of a landmass is the shortest line to it, and a landmass in the way would itself be a nearer landmass that herds cross to first. The search is brute force over coastal provinces (every coastal pair, one distance each through `UNITS.regionDistanceKm`); on the 204,000-point world it cost under the run-to-run noise of generation (24.4 s with routes against 24.8 s without).

**Dependence lag.** Crops can be domesticated long before farming becomes the main way of life. Maize was present in the Balsas valley by 8,700 cal BP (Piperno et al. 2009, *PNAS* 106: 5019–5024) but became a staple only after about 4,000 cal BP (Kennett et al. 2020, *Science Advances* 6: eaba3245), a lag of about 4,700 years, while the south-west Asian package was a staple within centuries. ## Ground, soil and terraces

`FARMING_GROUND` (`src/model/society/farming/ground`) cuts farm land on steep ground using the topography slope score (`CLASSIFICATION.computeSlopeScore`), so farming and the map's hill and mountain labels agree. The score is normalised to each planet's 95th-percentile slope, so "steep" is relative to the world.

Classified salt-pan regions receive the existing barren-soil factor of 0.08, regardless of their underlying fertility. The factor is area-weighted with the province's other regions; the pan classification covers whole regions, not a measured fraction within each region. It reduces crop and terrace fit, not farm capacity per farmed area.

- **Plough factor:** `1 − 0.92 · S([0.2, 0.6], slopeScore)`: losses start where topography starts calling land hill and reach the maximum where land over 0.5 km is mountain. Regions at or below 0.1 km elevation have factor 1. The province value is the area-weighted mean. `cropFit` (and so farm land share, post-farming mode and herd share) is multiplied by it; farm capacity and adoption are unchanged, to avoid double counting.
- **Terrace factor:** `1 − 0.92 · S([0.3, 1.0], slopeScore)`, never worse than the plough. `terraceFit = climateFit · (terrace − plough)` is the extra farm land terraces recover.
- **Tech gating:** `landAt` adds `S([0.5, 1.6], T̄) · terraceFit` to the farm potential, where T̄ is the average tech level: about −3000 (Yemen highland terraces) to −250 (southern Peru) on the Earth timeline.
- **Soil factor:** `clamp(fertility / SOIL_REF, 1 − SOIL_MAX_LOSS, 1)` with `SOIL_REF = 0.5` and the same 0.92 maximum loss, area-weighted per province from the region fertility of [rocks and soil](../rocks-and-soil.md). It multiplies the climate fit before the plough and terrace factors, so `cropFit` and `terraceFit` scale together. It only lowers share: average ground loses nothing, because the start-year targets were set without a soil term. Earth imports have no fertility field and use 1.
- `MAX_SLOPE_LOSS = 0.92` is upstream `slopeMaxLoss` (garden plots remain on the steepest ground).

Earth diagnostic effect of the slope factor alone: ArchaeoGLOBE sum 4.518 → 4.511, Pinhasi mean absolute error 877 → 880 years, HYDE population-share distance 0.5254 → 0.5268; about 290 provinces change post-farming mode and 17 get farming roughly 400 years later. The report's `ground` block carries the mean plough and terrace factors, the share of provinces below 0.5, the area-weighted mean soil factor and the share of provinces whose soil factor is below 0.5, hill and mountain percentages of land, and `meanShares` at T̄ 0.5, 1 and 1.6.

## Carrying density

**Mixed ways of life.** A province is not one way of life but a mix. `FARMING_WAY_OF_LIFE.wayOfLife` returns, per province, the share of its people who forage, farm and herd.

- **Land shares.** Each province has a farm land share `farmLand`, its crop fit (0–1), counted only where farm capacity beats forager density. It also has a herd land share `herdLand`, its pasture share `(1 − cropFit) · pasture · season`, counted only where herds exist (the migration origin's landmass, land farmed from its hearths, and land reached by a livestock voyage) and only where herd capacity beats forager density. The rest is forage land.
- **Switching over.** Farm land is foraged until the adoption year, then switches over along the 650-year adoption ramp `1 − e^(−t/650)`. Herd land does the same from the herd adoption year, which is the earlier of the pastoral adoption and the farming adoption wherever herds came with the crops.
- **People.** People per km² is the sum over the three: forage land × `d_f`, farm land × the farm density below, herd land × the herd density below. `FARMING_WAY_OF_LIFE.wayOfLife` returns that sum as `density` and divides each part by it for the shares.
- **Dominant practice.** `FARMING_WAY_OF_LIFE.modeOf` returns the largest share of the field, which the report and the ArchaeoGLOBE comparison read. Nothing switches at a threshold: every share grows smoothly with time and land.

**Land shares grow with farming age and technology** (`landAt` inside `FARMING_WAY_OF_LIFE.wayOfLife`, with the request's tech level):

- **Potential.** Each practice's potential is its climate fit (crop fit, or pasture fit on the animals' landmass), widened by the tech level's yield factor `y`: fit^(1/y), gated by `smoothstep(1, 2, capacity · y / forager density)`.
- **Yield factor.** `y` is 1 up to TL 3, then 1.4 at TL 4, 2 at TL 5, 2.5 at TL 6, 3.5 at TL 7 and 5.5 at TL 8. These are estimates from historical cereal yields relative to medieval levels.
- **Expansion.** Each practice holds that potential × the adoption ramp × `0.6 + 0.4 · (1 − e^(−t / 4,000))`, so a newly farmed province uses 60% of its potential and the rest is taken over the following millennia.
- **Overflow.** If farm and herd potential together exceed the province, both are scaled down to fit.
- **Foragers** keep whatever land is left.

**World field** (`FARMING_WAY_OF_LIFE.wayOfLife`, a world pass). It returns each province's way-of-life shares (forage, farm, herd) and `density` (people/km²) from the province's own land shares and per-practice densities, with no neighbour blending. Every consumer reads this one field: the Way of life map, the hover, capital eligibility, the TL starting points and caps, the report and the Earth diagnostic. Smoothing was tried and dropped: blending each province's land shares with its neighbours flattened dense cores and pushed the HYDE population-share distance from 0.56 to 0.95 (land from 0.14 to 0.47), while light blending barely removed the sharp edges.

## Earth diagnostic

`src/test/history-run/farming-earth.diagnostic.smoke.test.ts` asserts nothing and is skipped unless `HISTORY_FARMING_EARTH=1`. It imports the Earth world (seed 14963991, 204,000 points), and removes the climate model from every input: habitability is recomputed from the observed climate zones and vegetation, migration is re-run with the Earth origin hint on that habitability and the observed annual temperature and rainfall, and `FARMING.compute` reads observed monthly temperature, rainfall and GDD, the observed-hydrology PET and the world's terrain and rivers. Provinces with a missing observed month or GDD are excluded from the search and scoring masks and coverage is logged. It writes `stats/farming-earth/<UTC timestamp>.json` with the `sources.json` hashes and the Earth asset digests, or an incomplete-input record when a reference file or field is missing.

Scoring:

- **Hearth centres:** upstream's 14 curated centres (pinned `earth-history.mjs` `CENTRES`, onsets in years before 1950): distance to the nearest fired hearth, the farming arrival of the nearest habitable province within 350 km, the reference onset as `1950 − BP`, and the share with a fired hearth within 700 km. Upstream's AUC 0.755 on its own grid is context only.
- **Pinhasi dates:** each site matches the nearest habitable province centre within 350 km; signed error (model − site) median, p10, p90 and mean absolute error overall and in 1,000 km bands from the earliest site; share within `2 × cal_sd` and within ±500 years; front speed as the least-squares slope of date against distance from the earliest site for observed and modelled dates (published 0.6–1.3 km/yr).
- **ArchaeoGLOBE:** each province belongs to the region containing its centre. Per slice, the model's dominant way of life by settled area (ties farm, herd, forage) is compared with the experts' level: agreement as Widespread and as at least Common, a confusion table, the disagreeing regions with model shares, expert levels and mean capacities, and farming presence timing as a signed slice difference between the first slice with any model farm or herd area and the first slice the experts rate farming or pastoralism at least Minimal.
- **`worktreeComparable` block:** the same two reference sets scored with the definitions of the `fast-history-tech-level` worktree's `prehistory-earth` and `farming-earth` modules, so numbers are comparable across sessions. Each carries its definition string in the JSON. It sits beside, and does not replace, the scores above.
  - *Pinhasi:* a site matches when any reached habitable province centre lies within 250 km and takes the earliest farming year among them; sites with no such province are unmatched (including sites whose only neighbours are never reached). Reported: matched count, Spearman (Pearson on average ranks of site year against model year over matched sites), the signed median error and the median absolute error, both with the lower median (index `floor((n-1)/2)`). Our own block above matches the nearest province within 350 km and drops unreached ones, so its matched count and errors differ.
  - *ArchaeoGLOBE:* per slice, regions with at least one settled province count; the model's dominant way of life is the plurality by province count (ties farm, herd, forage), not settled area; the region agrees when the experts rate that way Widespread. Reported: per-slice agreement, and the sum and mean over the eight slices. It differs from our `widespreadAgreement` only through province-count against area weighting.

## Map modes

Population gains two children, offered only when `world.farming` is non-null (`hasPopulationModeData`):

- **Farming** colours `farmingYear` with the Migration gradient (`migrationColor`, scaled from the earliest arrival to the 98th percentile of reached provinces). Desolate and unreached provinces use Migration's neutral grey. It does not change with the timeline, so the dated uninhabited paint pass is skipped for it.
- **Way of life** blends the forage `#009E73`, farm `#E69F00` and herd `#CC79A7` colours (Okabe & Ito 2008 colour-blind-safe palette) by the people's shares (`FARMING_WAY_OF_LIFE.wayOfLife`) at the displayed date. Uninhabited land is neutral grey. The displayed date is the history scrubber's year, or the default start year of the active pipeline without a frame.

Hover in either child adds "Farming arrival" (or "Never"), "Farming hearth" ("Own hearth", the source province name, or an em dash) and "Way of life" (or "Uninhabited"), and is suppressed on desolate land.
