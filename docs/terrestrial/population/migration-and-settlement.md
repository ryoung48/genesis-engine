# Migration and settlement

Scope: `:history`. Source: `src/model/society/migration`, `src/model/society/migration/sea-links`, `src/model/pipelines/post-elevation`, `src/model/history/sim/engine/events/population`.

Every habitable province has one dated first settlement, computed once while the world is generated. The yearly census then replays those dates, so land nobody has reached yet is empty, and everything the simulation does afterwards reads the result.

Both history pipelines (`simulation` and `distribution`) read the same arrival field from `MIGRATION.compute`. The distribution engine replays it through `DISTRIBUTION_SETTLEMENT` and builds its cradle schedule with `MIGRATION.cradles`; the same pass also sets each province's `areaKm2`. Earth imports compute it too, from the Omo Kibish origin hint, and keep it in `population.arrivalYear` rather than in `historyMigration`.

## Arrival field

`MIGRATION.compute` runs one earliest-arrival search from a single origin. The origin is settled at 79,999 BCE. Settlers within 1,000 km of it spread from the start; settlers beyond that leave no earlier than 65,000 BCE, the time modern humans left Africa (about 70 to 60 thousand years ago). The 1,000 km homeland radius is the scale of the origin's own highland and rift region (Omo Kibish to the Ethiopian highlands), which was occupied well before the dispersal; a larger radius lets the front pass the Levant before its 55 to 45 thousand year window, because the model's early speed leaves no room for a head start.

- **Suitability** is a province's habitability per land region, divided by the 95th percentile of that value over non-desolate provinces and clamped to 1. The percentile keeps a few exceptional provinces from slowing the whole world.
- **Origin** is the province in the most habitable land component whose neighbourhood scores highest on suitability, weighted by area and by the share of that area with water access; seeded ties break on a hash.
- **Land links** join adjacent provinces on the same landmass. Province adjacency also contains links across water, which are ignored here; water is crossed only by sea and shelf links, and only between different land components (below), so a bay on one landmass never becomes a faster route than walking round it. **Desolate land is transit only.** Settlers can walk across a desolate province but never settle it: an edge that touches desolate land is timed at suitability 0, the floor speed `0.55 × 0.3` km a year, with no river bonus, and the province keeps the sentinel arrival year, stays out of `order`, the waves, cradles and population, and has an infinite continuous arrival. Without this, a belt of desolate provinces (the Verkhoyansk–Chersky ranges on Earth) split a landmass in two and the far side was reached only over a sea link. The search therefore uses its own land components, computed over all land provinces desolate ones included (`walkGraph`); a landmass is a component of that graph, and sea and shelf links join only different components. `GenesisProvinces.landmassId` keeps its meaning (non-desolate land only, −1 for desolate), because farming, held-core budgets and the report read it. A settled province whose route ran through desolate land reports as its predecessor the nearest settled ancestor, so the run's settlement chain, the routes and the display tree never see a desolate province; its edge kind and arrival tier are those of the walk, and its contact year passes through like land. Settlers cover 0.55 kilometres a year times a speed factor `0.3 + 0.7 × mean suitability`, and 1.25 times that along rivers and lakes. The floor of 0.3 stands for foragers crossing cold, arid or otherwise marginal land at about a third of the pace of rich land; the old response (suitability itself, floored at 0.05) made a tundra crossing take about ten times longer than a savanna one and delayed Beringia by roughly ten thousand years. The early rate was lowered from 0.7 to 0.55 to compensate, calibrated on the Earth reference dates: the Levant, India, Laos and Sunda stay in window while Siberia and Europe come 10 to 25 thousand years closer to theirs. From 10000 BCE the speed rises smoothly (a smoothstep in the year) until, at 1500, it reaches fifty kilometres a year times the speed factor, about ninety times the early rate. The rise stands for the spread of farming and then of faster travel. A crossing is timed by the progress integral of the speed, so a walk that starts before the rise is accelerated as it goes. The early rate and the departure year are calibrated to the Earth dispersal maps (Stringer; Wells; National Geographic Genographic): Australia about 50 thousand years ago, Europe 45 to 35, Alaska 20 to 15, South America 15 to 12.
- **Sea links** join the nearest shores across open water. The corridor length is the water between the two coasts: the path through ocean cell centres, with the first and last legs from land centre to ocean centre counted only to their midpoint, where the Voronoi coast lies. Ocean cells are much larger than land cells in the deep ocean, so the coast placement is only as precise as the land cells beside it; the report still counts links within one mesh spacing of the first three knots below. A crossing takes `max(departure, capability year) + 100 + length / 10` years.

The arrival field is the foragers' front. Farming arrival and way of life are a separate generation-time field that waits for it and never changes it: see [farming arrival and way of life](farming.md).

A crossing cannot start before the capability year for its length, which rises smoothly with length: linear in the logarithm of the length between these points, constant below the first and above the last.

| Corridor | Capability year | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 130 km | 65,000 BCE | Timor to Sahul, about 90 km, crossed 65 to 50 thousand years ago |
| 185 km | 5000 BCE | Cuba and the Greater Antilles, settled about 5000 to 3500 BCE |
| 850 km | 1000 BCE | Lapita voyaging from Island Melanesia into Fiji, Tonga and Samoa |
| 3,000 km | 800 CE | Open-ocean voyaging into East Polynesia and Hawaii, about 1000 to 1300 CE |

A large landmass is easier to find than a small island, so the length used for the capability year is the corridor divided by `1 + 2 × log10(destination landmass area / 1,000,000 km²)`, never below 1. Landmasses under a million square kilometres are unaffected, so Hawaii, Tahiti and New Zealand keep their dates; a continent across 3,000 km of ocean behaves like a shorter crossing. The factor 2 is calibrated: with it Earth's reference dates still fit while continents on generated worlds start filling earlier.

**Small islands settle late.** (Landmass here means a land component, so land joined across desolate ground counts as one.) A dead-end small island is one that no later-settled landmass depends on for its own route (the origin's landmass and any landmass on the way to a larger one do not count; a route back to an earlier-settled landmass does not count either). Its earliest settlement year is `-11700 + f × (-500 − (-11700))`, where `f` is the linear fraction of `log(30,000 km² / area)` over `log(30,000 / 2,000)`, clamped to 0 to 1: landmasses of 30,000 km² and above are unaffected, and the date reaches 500 BCE at 2,000 km² and below. Every province of the island is delayed by the same amount, so the order inside it is kept; provinces elsewhere that were routed through it fall back to an earlier ancestor. Pleistocene foragers crossed to Sahul and Wallacea but left no secure trace on small Mediterranean islands before the Holocene (Cherry 1990; Cherry and Leppard 2018), so island chains that lead on to a large landmass are left alone and only the dead ends wait. 30,000 km² is just above Sardinia and Cyprus-scale islands, 11,700 BCE is the start of the Holocene (Walker et al. 2009), and 2,000 km² with 500 BCE puts the smallest islands (Canaries, Balearics) in the late Holocene. The rule is a crude stand-in: it also delays New Ireland and the Solomons, which were in fact settled in the Pleistocene along intervisible chains, and Malta, which here sits on Sicily's landmass.

The knots are calibrated to these dated settlements, and there are no tiers: the arrival tier recorded for reports is the number of the knot interval the crossing falls in. A corridor measured a few tens of kilometres wrong moves the date smoothly rather than across a gate.

- **Shelf links** join shores across ocean regions at or above −60 m. A shelf link is crossed at land speed with no sea delay when the departure is before 9500 BCE, and is an ordinary sea link afterwards. Sea level stayed below −60 m for most of 80 to 12 thousand years ago and passed it near 11.5 thousand years ago (Lambeck et al., 2014, PNAS 111:15296). One fixed threshold stands in for a level that ran from about −40 m to −120 m, so links that exist only at the last glacial maximum are left out. The shelf crossing time is the earlier of the land and sea times; because a link only ever closes, leaving later never helps and the search stays valid.

**Calibration result.** Against the 49 Earth reference dates (`src/test/earth/migration-compare.diagnostic.smoke.test.ts`) the three changes take the field from 15 within window and a mean absolute error of 9,866 years to 17 within and 5,674. Sunda and Sahul hold at 6 of 10, the Mediterranean islands move from about 50 thousand years early to within 1 to 5 thousand years of their windows (Crete and Sardinia inside), and Pacific, Caribbean, Levant, India and Laos keep their dates. Southern Africa (about 59,000 BCE) is limited by the early spread rate, not by the departure year.

**Desolate transit result.** Before desolate land was walkable, the Okhotsk coast, Kolyma and Chukotka were separate landmasses behind a desolate belt, joined to Siberia only by a 183 km sea link that opens near 10000 BCE, so NE Siberia arrived about 1415 BCE (target 32,000–28,000 BCE) and the Americas 4 to 8 thousand years late. With transit the same diagnostic goes from 17 within window and a mean absolute error of 5,674 years to 20 within and 5,085. NE Siberia arrives about 37,600 BCE (5.6 thousand years early), Alaska about 32,200 BCE, Mexico, the Amazon and Peru fall inside their windows, and the Americas go from 0 to 3 of 8 within with a mean error of 4,555 (from 5,560). Alaska, Oregon and eastern North America now overshoot their windows, by 8 to 14 thousand years, which the early rate cannot trade away without losing Siberia. Only Iceland among the other sites moved (about 100 years earlier). The share of provinces settled by 15000 BCE rises from 0.63 to 0.83 and no province is left unreached.

Each province keeps its arrival year (an integer; 2,147,483,647 when the search never reaches it or reaches it after 2025), its predecessor, the kind of link that reached it and the arrival tier. `migrationWave` is `(arrival + 79,999) / (2025 + 79,999)` clamped to the unit interval, and −1 for unreached land. Culture, religion and government read the wave unchanged; population falloff does too, except at the arrival-driven 867 start (below).

Each reached province also has a contact year: the first year from which routine voyages span every sea crossing on its migration path from the origin. A land link, or a shelf link before the shelf closes, passes the predecessor's contact year on; a sea link takes the later of the predecessor's contact year and `seaCapabilityYear` of the raw crossing. Arrival over sea divides the crossing by `targetReach`, because a large landmass is an easy target for a one-way voyage; contact uses the raw distance, because a lasting connection needs the voyage made routinely. On the verification world the later continents are reached through single 1,718–2,071 km crossings, contacted between about 5 and 270 CE, against 460 km or less inside the origin's network. Unreached land gets the sentinel. Statehood uses it through `TARGET_PROFILE.contactShare` ([land status](../politics/land-status-and-targets.md)).

Unreached land is **uninhabited**, never desolate: it stays part of the world, empty until its settlers arrive or for good.

## Cradles

`MIGRATION.cradles` selects up to eight independent agricultural origins, reviewed every 100 years from 3300 BCE. A province qualifies once it has been settled 2,000 years (the time for a region to support a state) and lies at least `2000 × radius / 6371` km from every earlier cradle, so cradles keep to separate regions at any planet size. Candidates rank by site score times `min(settled years / 2000, 2)` times `min(distance to nearest cradle / spacing, 2)`. The site score is `PLACEMENT.nationPlacementScore` for a one-province state.

## Settlement in the run

`POPULATION.runPopulation` settles every province whose arrival year has come, in arrival order, before the census grows anything:

- the province changes from uninhabited to stateless, dated 1 January of its arrival year;
- it receives rural population from its predecessor, `predecessorRural × w / (w + w_pred)` with `w` the habitability, so population is conserved;
- it takes the predecessor's knowledge.

Cultures and religions cover every habitable province, so a province already has its culture and a name when settlers arrive, and land that is never reached still has both. Uninhabited provinces have no population, are skipped by the yearly growth and development passes, and cannot be founded on or absorbed.

## Starting settlement and states

At the start the settled land is the provinces with `arrival ≤ start year`, where the start year is the planet's own year for the requested [tech level](tech-levels.md#the-planets-own-start-year). History's calendar calls it 867, and arrival years are shifted to match. `MIGRATION.heldCore` takes `round(h × settled)` of them as state-held, with `h` the [tech level](tech-levels.md#state-held-land-and-realm-sizes)'s state-held share (46.35% at the default 2.35), grown from the active cradles plus core seeds spaced a cradle spacing apart over all settled land, with each landmass's count weighted by contact age; the rest of the settled world starts stateless. If nothing seeds the search, it starts at the origin.

Population at this start replaces the wave falloff with farming carrying density: each settled province's weight is `FARMING_WAY_OF_LIFE.wayOfLife` density at the planet's start year × area, renormalised to the total from the [tech level](tech-levels.md#world-population) times the settled habitability score, so a province's share follows its way of life, its yield and how long it has farmed (see [farming](farming.md#carrying-density)). This replaces the earlier settlement-age factor `1 − exp(−age / 4,400)`, which counted years since first arrival and so gave long-settled forager land full density. The distribution pipeline and other eras keep the wave falloff; they have no start year to evaluate density at. See [land status and targets](../politics/land-status-and-targets.md). Other eras keep their wave percentiles.

## Report

The Population map mode has Density, Migration, Development and Knowledge children in the overlay controls (see [Density colors](#density-colors) below). A child is offered only when the displayed world carries its data (`getAvailablePopulationModes`): Migration needs the arrival field, Development and Knowledge need the per-province development and knowledge arrays, and Density needs only population. If the selected child becomes unavailable (for example after loading another world), the selection falls back to Density. Migration colors the **arrival ramp**, not the arrival years: `MIGRATION.rampYears` ranks the reached provinces by arrival year (ties by province index) and spaces the ranks evenly, so the origin keeps 79,999 BCE and the k-th of the other n − 1 gets `ceil(−65,000 + (k − 1) / max(1, n − 2) × (1300 + 65,000))`. The ramp is a display field derived from the arrival years, which are unchanged; it has the same order as the search but not its dates. It ends at 1300 CE, the end of the Polynesian settlements in the Earth diagnostic's reference table (New Zealand 1250 to 1300). The color runs from blue (earliest) through green and yellow to red (latest), scaled from the earliest ramp year to the 98th percentile of reached provinces. Hover details show the arrival year and the ramp year while this mode is active. Unreached and desolate land stays neutral. This mode is available without debug map modes, on procedural worlds and Earth imports alike. Earth imports offer Density and Migration but not Development or Knowledge, because their history is real data and no simulated development or knowledge field exists for them.

Population also offers Farming and Way of life children when the world carries the farming field (see [farming](farming.md)).

The detailed history report records the origin, cradles, link counts per kind, sea length quantiles, the gate-sensitivity counts, the suitability scale, and the shares settled at the start, uninhabited at the start and never reached.

### Density colors

Density colors the province population divided by the province land area in inhabitants per km² (`getProvincePopulationDensity`), whatever unit system the hover text uses. The scale is fixed and absolute, so maps of different worlds and dates are directly comparable; it is never rescaled to the world's own maximum. The bins and colors are those of the HYDE 3.1 population density legend (Klein Goldewijk, Beusen & Janssen 2010), with the colors sampled from the published legend. Bins are half-open on the continuous density, lower bound inclusive:

| inh/km² | Legend label | Color |
|---|---|---|
| [0, 1) | < 1 | white at 0, fading linearly to `#fbe15e` at 1 |
| [1, 6) | 1 - 5 | `#fbe15e` |
| [6, 11) | 6 - 10 | `#fccb47` |
| [11, 31) | 11 - 30 | `#f5ab30` |
| [31, 51) | 31 - 50 | `#df8421` |
| [51, 101) | 51 - 100 | `#a95524` |
| [101, 500) | 101 - 500 | `#862e1a` |
| [500, ∞) | > 500 | `#5a0f0d` |

Below 1 inh/km² the color is not a flat bin: it runs from white at zero to the 1–5 yellow at 1, so empty and near-empty land reads as white. HYDE's legend uses a flat grey here; the fade departs from it. Desolate land keeps its separate warm neutral grey, so uninhabitable land stays distinguishable from sparsely settled land. The Difference variant is unchanged and still scales to the largest absolute difference.
