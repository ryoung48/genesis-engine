What you're looking at

Every polygon here was traced from Imperium Universalis's hand-drawn provinces.bmp — a 6400×2560 bitmap that covers only the Old World, in no known projection (the top edge visibly curves through Siberia and the map cuts off mid-continent on three sides) — then warped onto real latitude/longitude. Unlike the extended-timeline extraction, there were no province-id correspondences to fit against: IU copied vanilla's definition.csv but reassigned every id to a brand-new location (id 1, nominally "Stockholm", is a small island near Sicily), which poisons any id- or name-based matching beyond repair.

The projection was instead recovered from geography alone: __ANCHOR_COUNT__ hand-placed landmark anchors — straits, river deltas, capes, and inland lakes read off the terrain bitmap — bootstrap a thin-plate spline, which a coarse-to-fine ICP pass then snaps onto Natural Earth's land-50m coastline (match caps shrinking 8°→1.2°, with the mod's 1-px river-provinces excluded so the Nile can't get glued to the Red Sea). The thin dark line is that real coastline: where a tinted shape's edge tracks it, the recovered warp is doing its job. Tints only distinguish neighbors; they carry no meaning; flat gray marks impassable wasteland (the giant impassable fillers IU paints over map-border oceans are omitted). Interior accuracy is interpolated between coasts and lake anchors — there is no inland reference to do better with.
