What you're looking at

Every polygon here was traced from the mod's hand-drawn provinces.bmp — a bitmap in no known projection — then warped onto real latitude/longitude. The projection was recovered empirically: ~3,000 provinces keep their vanilla EU4 ids, so their pixel centroids were matched to known Earth coordinates and a thin-plate spline fitted through the correspondences. From there, two ICP passes refine it: first each kept province's entire boundary — not just its centroid — is matched against that same id's reference shape, then a second pass snaps every continent's edge onto Natural Earth's global land coastline (land-50m).

The thin dark outlines are the old map — vanilla EU4's provinces from eu4.json, the reference the projection was fitted against. Where a tinted shape and a dark outline track each other, the recovered warp is doing its job; where they part ways, the mod genuinely redrew the geography (NE Siberia, the arctic). Tints only distinguish neighbors; they carry no meaning. Provinces the mod moved away from their vanilla location (the relocated toggle) were excluded from the fit and are drawn where the mod put them.

Regenerate with scripts/build-mod-province-geojson.py · output geo-explorer/public/eu4-extended-timeline.json. This is the direct output of that step, before the grid-snap alignment pass.
