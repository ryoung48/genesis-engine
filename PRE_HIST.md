# Pre-Historic & Ancient Era Analysis
## Nation Sizes, Statehood, and Stateless Societies: Paleolithic to Iron Age

> Research compiled from academic literature (Service 1962, Diamond 1997, Scott 2017, Turchin/Seshat 2018,
> PNAS territorial expansion studies) cross-referenced with EU4 extended-timeline data where coverage begins (~350 AD).
> Quantitative estimates carry substantial uncertainty; confidence intervals noted where available.

---

## 1. Overview: The Statehood Spectrum

Before formal states existed, human land use passed through a spectrum of organization:

| Category | Definition | % Habitable Land (est.) |
|---|---|---|
| **State** | Centralized hierarchy, taxation, standing army, bureaucracy, territorial sovereignty | 0% → 83% (Paleolithic → today) |
| **Chiefdom** | Hereditary leadership, tribute economy, multi-village but no full bureaucracy | 0% → peak ~15% (Neolithic–Bronze) |
| **Tribe** | Kin-based, council leadership, semi-permanent territory, no formal sovereignty | 0% → peak ~40% (Neolithic–Iron) |
| **Band** | 25–100 people, egalitarian, mobile, no territorial claim beyond seasonal range | ~100% → 0% (Paleolithic → today) |
| **Uninhabited / unclaimed** | Habitable but effectively beyond any group's regular range | varies by era and region |

The EU4 data (from 500 AD onward) collapses chiefdoms and tribes into "tribal" government and tracks the
state/tribal/uninhabited split precisely. The pre-500 AD picture must be reconstructed from archaeology.

## 2. Paleolithic (c.300,000–10,000 BC)

### Political Organization
Pure band-level societies. No states, no chiefdoms, no formal territories.

### Group Sizes
| Unit | Size | Territory |
|---|---|---|
| Nuclear family unit | 5–10 | — |
| Band (foraging unit) | 25–50 (range: 15–100) | ~80–500 km² |
| Macro-band / dialect tribe | ~150 (Dunbar's number) | ~2,000–5,000 km² |
| Regional network | ~500–1,500 | — |

- The 25–50 band size is robustly supported across ethnographic and archaeological evidence
  (Kelly 1995, Binford 2001).
- Territory scales with ~0.75 power law relative to group size: larger groups are more land-efficient
  but still require vast ranges in low-productivity environments.

### Statehood / Settlement Breakdown (est.)
| Category | % Habitable Land |
|---|---|
| State-controlled | 0% |
| Tribal / chiefdom | 0% |
| Inhabited by bands | ~30–60% of *habitable* land (varies by glacial period) |
| Effectively uninhabited habitable land | ~40–70% |

- During glacial maxima (~20,000 BP), large portions of currently habitable land were under ice or desert.
- At the Pleistocene/Holocene boundary (~10,000 BC) world population was ~2–8 million, spread across
  all continents except Antarctica.
- No evidence of territorial sovereignty or exclusive land ownership.

### Population
| Date | World Pop (estimate) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 100,000 BC | ~100,000–300,000 | Genetic bottleneck evidence |
| 50,000 BC | ~500,000–1M | Demographic models |
| 10,000 BC | 2–8M | Standard archaeological consensus |

## 3. Mesolithic / Epi-Paleolithic (c.10,000–7,000 BC)

### Political Organization
Continuing band societies, but with first signs of semi-sedentism in resource-rich environments
(coastal, river delta, forest edge). No formal states or chiefdoms except at exceptional sites.

### Key Developments
- **Natufian culture** (Levant, ~12,500–9,500 BC): first semi-sedentary villages of 50–150 people;
  possible hereditary status but not chiefdoms in the full sense.
- **Jōmon culture** (Japan, ~14,000 BC): complex hunter-gatherers, semi-sedentary, population densities
  higher than continental contemporaries.
- European Mesolithic: continued small mobile bands (~200,000 people in Europe c.9,000 BC).

### Statehood / Settlement Breakdown (est.)
| Category | % Habitable Land |
|---|---|
| State-controlled | 0% |
| Chiefdom / proto-tribal | <1% (exceptional sites only) |
| Inhabited by bands/tribes | ~40–70% |
| Uninhabited habitable | ~30–60% |

### Population
| Date | World Pop |
|---|---|
| 10,000 BC | 2–8M |
| 8,000 BC | 5–10M |

## 4. Neolithic (c.7,000–3,000 BC)

### Political Organization
First permanent villages, tribal societies with hereditary leadership emerging. Chiefdoms appear
in the late Neolithic in the Fertile Crescent, Europe (megaliths), and China.

### Settlement / Polity Sizes
| Type | Population | Territory (km²) | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small farming village | 50–200 | 5–50 | Ain Ghazal (Jordan), c.7,200 BC |
| Large Neolithic village | 300–1,000 | 50–200 | Çatalhöyük (~6,700 BC): ~600–800 people |
| Chiefdom cluster | 1,000–10,000 | 500–5,000 | Late Neolithic Europe, Yangshao China |
| Proto-urban center | 5,000–20,000 | — | Eridu (Mesopotamia), earliest levels |

- **Çatalhöyük** (~6,700–5,700 BC, Turkey): ~8,000 people at peak; egalitarian (no evidence of
  hereditary elites); spatially dense (~13 ha), no central plaza or administrative buildings.
- **Jiangzhai** (China, ~4,000 BC): moated village, ~200 structures, population ~500–1,000.
- European megaliths (Avebury, Carnac, ~3,500 BC): evidence of chiefdom-level organization capable
  of mobilizing large labor forces without formal state apparatus.

### Statehood / Settlement Breakdown (est.)
| Category | % Habitable Land | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| State-controlled | 0% | No states yet |
| Chiefdom territory | ~2–5% | Fertile Crescent, S. Europe, NW China |
| Tribal / village | ~20–40% | Expanding farming zones |
| Band / forager | ~15–30% | Retreating but still dominant in forests/steppes |
| Uninhabited habitable | ~25–50% | Americas, sub-Saharan, Oceania largely empty |

### Population
| Date | World Pop |
|---|---|
| 7,000 BC | 5–10M |
| 5,000 BC | 15–20M |
| 3,000 BC | 30–50M |

## 5. Chalcolithic / Copper Age (c.4,500–3,200 BC)

### Political Organization
First genuine proto-states appear in Mesopotamia (~3,700 BC) and the Indus Valley (~3,300 BC).
Chiefdoms widespread across the Old World. Americas and sub-Saharan Africa remain tribal/band.

### Key Polities and Sizes
| Polity | Date | Territory (km²) | Population (est.) | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uruk (earliest city-state) | ~3,700 BC | ~1,000–3,000 | 20,000–50,000 | Proto-state |
| Predynastic Egypt (Upper) | ~3,500 BC | ~50,000 | ~500,000 | Chiefdom → proto-state |
| Mehrgarh / proto-Indus | ~3,500 BC | Regional | unknown | Chiefdom cluster |
| Tripolye culture (Ukraine) | ~4,000 BC | ~2–3 km² per "mega-site" | 10,000–15,000/site | Complex chiefdom |

- Tripolye mega-sites (Ukraine/Moldova) are among the largest known pre-state settlements: up to
  3.5 km², ~10,000–15,000 inhabitants, yet without evidence of a ruling class or palace economy.
- The Uruk expansion (~3,500–3,100 BC) represents the world's first documented economic colonialism —
  Mesopotamian trading colonies established across the Fertile Crescent and Anatolia.

### Statehood / Settlement Breakdown (est.)
| Category | % Habitable Land |
|---|---|
| State / proto-state | ~1–3% (Mesopotamia, Nile valley) |
| Chiefdom | ~5–10% (Europe, Indus, NE Africa) |
| Tribal / village | ~25–40% |
| Band / forager | ~15–25% |
| Uninhabited habitable | ~25–40% |

### Population
~30–50M world population (~3,200 BC)

## 6. Bronze Age (c.3,200–1,200 BC)

### Political Organization
First true states with writing, taxation, standing armies, and territorial sovereignty.
City-states dominant in Mesopotamia; regional empires in Egypt, Hittites, Shang China.
Vast stateless hinterlands remain — only a small fraction of world land area is under formal control.

### Polity Sizes
| Polity | Date | Territory (km²) | Population (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mesopotamian city-state (typical) | 2800–2300 BC | ~1,000–5,000 | 20,000–100,000 |
| Akkadian Empire (peak) | ~2,250 BC | ~650,000 | ~1–2M under influence |
| Old Kingdom Egypt | ~2,500 BC | ~1,000,000 | ~1–1.5M |
| Indus Valley Civilization | ~2,500 BC | ~1,300,000 | ~1–5M |
| Hittite Empire (peak) | ~1,300 BC | ~500,000–1,000,000 | ~1–2M |
| Shang Dynasty China | ~1,200 BC | ~830,000 | ~3–5M (sphere of influence) |
| Mycenaean palace-state | ~1,400 BC | ~5,000–20,000 | 50,000–200,000 |

### City Spacing (Mesopotamia)
Early Dynastic Mesopotamia (~2,800 BC): 13–30 independent city-states in ~25,900 km²,
giving ~860–2,000 km² per polity on average. Cities spaced ~25–35 km apart (one day's travel).

### Statehood / Settlement Breakdown
| Category | % Habitable Land | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| State-controlled | ~5–10% | Mesopotamia, Egypt, Indus, Shang China, Hittites |
| Chiefdom | ~10–15% | Europe (Bronze Age), SE Asia, sub-Saharan |
| Tribal / pastoral | ~30–40% | Steppes, forests, Americas, Oceania |
| Band / forager | ~10–20% | Arctic, deep forest, remote regions |
| Uninhabited habitable | ~15–30% | Americas, Oceania, Central Africa largely empty |

### Population
| Date | World Pop |
|---|---|
| 3,200 BC | ~50M |
| 2,000 BC | ~100M |
| 1,200 BC | ~100M (Bronze Age Collapse disrupts growth) |

### Key Note: Bronze Age Collapse (~1,200 BC)
The Late Bronze Age collapse wiped out most palace-state civilizations in the eastern Mediterranean
simultaneously (Mycenae, Hittites, Ugarit, etc.). This represents the largest regression in statehood
coverage in recorded history — an estimated 30–40% of controlled territory reverted to chiefdom or
tribal organization within ~50 years.

## 7. Iron Age (c.1,200–200 BC)

### Political Organization
Recovery and expansion of state organization. Territorial kingdoms replace city-states as the dominant
form. First mega-empires appear (Achaemenid Persia, Qin/Han China, later Rome). Yet even at the peak
of these empires, the majority of world land area remained outside formal state control.

### Major Polity Sizes
| Polity | Date | Territory (km²) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical Greek city-state (polis) | 600–300 BC | 100–5,000 | Athens: ~2,500 km², population ~250,000 |
| Assyrian Empire (peak) | ~660 BC | ~1,400,000 | First empire to use systematic deportation |
| Achaemenid Persian Empire (peak) | ~500 BC | ~5,500,000 | Largest empire to that date; ~10% of world land |
| Maurya Empire (peak) | ~250 BC | ~5,000,000 | Most of South Asia |
| Qin/Han China (unified) | ~200 BC | ~4,000,000 | ~400–600M inhabitants by Han peak |
| Warring States China (pre-unification) | 475–221 BC | ~100,000–500,000 per state | 7 major states, dozens of minor |
| Roman Republic (200 BC) | 200 BC | ~400,000 | Still expanding |

### % of World Land Under Formal States (Iron Age estimates)
| Date | State% | Chiefdom/Tribal% | Band% | Uninhabited% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,200 BC (post-collapse) | ~5–8% | ~20–30% | ~20–30% | ~35–45% |
| 800 BC | ~8–12% | ~25–35% | ~15–25% | ~30–40% |
| 500 BC | ~12–18% | ~25–30% | ~15–20% | ~30–40% |
| 200 BC | ~15–22% | ~25–30% | ~10–15% | ~30–40% |

*These estimates draw on Turchin et al. (2018) Seshat database, Manning (2013), and
territorial extent data from Taagepera (1978, 1979) empire-size studies.*

### "Against the Grain" Perspective (Scott 2017)
James Scott's analysis emphasizes that formal states controlled *narrow corridors* — primarily
river valleys and coastal plains — even at their apparent peak. Mountain, forest, steppe, and
wetland populations actively evaded state taxation and conscription. The "barbarian zones" of
Germanic tribes, Celtic peoples, Scythian steppe nomads, and sub-Saharan societies were not
simply pre-state primitives but often *ex-state populations* who fled state control.

EU4 data from 500 AD onward confirms this: even at the 1444 snapshot, 39% of provinces remain
without a formal owner, and 19% are under tribal governance.

## 8. Connecting Pre-History to EU4 Data (500 AD Bridge)

The EU4 data begins at ~350 AD, giving us a calibration point for the Iron Age endpoint.
At 500 AD (Late Antiquity), the EU4 dataset records:

- **State-owned provinces**: 28% of total
- **Tribal-owned provinces**: 19%
- **Uninhabited / unclaimed**: 53%

This 500 AD snapshot likely *understates* real statehood coverage because:
1. The Roman Empire (collapsed ~476 AD) had controlled far more territory at its peak (~200–400 AD)
2. The Han Dynasty and Gupta Empire are similarly not well-represented in the EU4 province system
3. EU4 provinces concentrate on Eurasia and North Africa; the Americas, Oceania, and much of sub-Saharan Africa
   are modeled as "uninhabited" even though they had real populations

Correcting for these biases, the true picture at 200 AD (Iron Age endpoint) was likely:
- State: ~20–30% of habitable land
- Tribal/chiefdom: ~30–40%
- Band/forager: ~10–20%
- Genuinely uninhabited habitable: ~15–30%

## 9. Unified Timeline: Band → Tribe → Chiefdom → State

### A Note on "Uninhabited" vs. "Unlivable"

All percentages below refer to **habitable land only** — i.e., land where humans could plausibly
survive given the technology of the era. Truly uninhabitable zones (Antarctic ice sheet, deep
Sahara core, Greenland interior, high-altitude glaciers) are excluded from the denominator
throughout. The EU4 dataset confirms this: it contains ~4,195 provinces representing the habitable
world, and truly unlivable zones are simply not provinces at all. The EU4 "wasteland" provinces
(tagged as hard to colonize) are marginal but inhabitable — Rub' al Khali, Siberian tundra,
Namibian desert — and are included in the 4,195 count with native culture data.

**"Uninhabited" in the table below = habitable land with no humans regularly present.**
This number was genuinely large in early prehistory (most of the Americas, Oceania, sub-Saharan
Africa, and interior Asia were beyond the range of any known population group).

| Era | Date | World Pop | State% | Tribal/Chiefdom% | Band% | Uninhabited (habitable)% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paleolithic | 50,000 BC | ~0.5M | 0 | 0 | 40–60 | 40–60 |
| Late Paleolithic | 10,000 BC | 2–8M | 0 | 0 | 40–60 | 40–60 |
| Mesolithic | 8,000 BC | 5–10M | 0 | <1 | 40–65 | 35–55 |
| Neolithic | 5,000 BC | 15–20M | 0 | 5–15 | 30–50 | 35–60 |
| Chalcolithic | 3,500 BC | 30–40M | 1–3 | 10–20 | 20–35 | 40–55 |
| Bronze Age | 2,000 BC | ~100M | 5–10 | 20–30 | 15–25 | 35–50 |
| Iron Age (early) | 800 BC | ~120M | 8–12 | 25–35 | 15–20 | 30–40 |
| Iron Age (peak) | 200 BC | ~150M | 15–22 | 25–30 | 10–15 | 30–40 |
| Late Antiquity | 500 AD | ~200M | ~34* | ~22* | ~3 | ~44* |
| High Medieval | 1000 AD | ~300M | ~48* | ~19* | ~1 | ~33* |
| Late Medieval | 1444 AD | ~400M | ~51* | ~22* | <1 | ~27* |
| Early Modern | 1550 AD | ~500M | ~57* | ~19* | <1 | ~24* |
| Industrial | 1836 AD | ~1.2B | ~84* | ~7* | 0 | ~9* |
| Modern | 2025 AD | 8.1B | ~100* | ~0* | 0 | ~0* |

*\* = EU4 land-province data (3,482 land provinces; sea/lake tiles excluded).*
*Pre-500 AD: estimated from population density and archaeological coverage.*
*At 2025, every one of the 3,482 habitable land provinces has a state owner — consistent*
*with the real world where all habitable land is sovereign territory of some nation.*

## 10. Implications for Genesis Engine Era Calibration

### `settlementFraction` and `statehoodFraction`

These parameters in `eras.ts` control:
- `settlementFraction`: fraction of **non-desolate** provinces that are inhabited at all
- `statehoodFraction`: fraction of inhabited provinces under formal states

**Important alignment:** Genesis "desolate" provinces map to truly uninhabitable terrain
(ice caps, hyper-arid cores, etc.) — equivalent to land *outside* the EU4 province system.
`settlementFraction` therefore applies to the habitable world only, exactly matching the
EU4 province universe. The calibration table below uses EU4 ownership ratios directly.

Cross-referencing the unified timeline with current values:

| Era | Current `settlementFraction` | Evidence-based | Current `statehoodFraction` | Evidence-based |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `paleolithic` | 0.30 | **0.10–0.20** (vast empty continent) | 0 | ✓ 0 |
| `neolithic` | 0.55 | **0.25–0.40** (farming pockets only) | 0.15 | **0.02–0.08** (tiny chiefdom zone) |
| `bronze` | 0.72 | **0.40–0.55** (Eurasia+Egypt states, rest tribal) | 0.30 | **0.15–0.25** (state land ≈ 5–10% of total) |
| `iron` | 0.85 | **0.60–0.70** (EU4 750 AD: 64% settled) | 0.55 | **0.65–0.68** (EU4 750: state/(state+tribal)=67%) |
| `lateMedieval` | 1.00 | **~0.80–0.90** (EU4 1350: 73% settled; Eurocentric bias inflates uninhabited) | 1.00 | **0.72–0.75** (EU4 1350: state/(state+tribal)=74%) |
| `earlyModern` | 1.00 | **~0.85–0.95** | 1.00 | **0.75–0.80** (EU4 1550: state/(state+tribal)=75%) |
| `industrial` | 1.00 | ✓ 1.00 | 1.00 | **0.90–0.93** (EU4 1836: state/(state+tribal)=92%) |
| `information` | 1.00 | ✓ 1.00 | 1.00 | ✓ 1.00 (EU4 2025: 100% of settled is state) |

### Key Gaps Between Current Model and Historical Evidence

1. **`paleolithic.settlementFraction` (0.30) is too high.** At 10,000 BC, world population was 2–8M
   across ~130M km² of habitable land. Density ≈ 0.015–0.06 people/km². Even assuming generous
   territorial bands of 25 people per 500 km², only ~10–20% of habitable provinces would have any
   regular human presence. **Recommend: 0.10–0.15.**

2. **`neolithic.settlementFraction` (0.55) is too high.** Early Neolithic farming was confined to
   river valleys and selected ecological zones. By 5,000 BC, farming cultures covered perhaps 20–30%
   of Eurasia and none of the Americas, Oceania, or sub-Saharan Africa. **Recommend: 0.25–0.35.**

3. **`neolithic.statehoodFraction` (0.15) is too high for early Neolithic but reasonable for late.**
   True chiefdoms only emerge ~4,000 BC. For the era as a whole, 0.05–0.10 is more accurate.

4. **`bronze.settlementFraction` (0.72) and `statehoodFraction` (0.30) are both plausible** if the
   era targets ~1,500 BC. The Indus, Shang, and Hittite worlds were extensive by then.

5. **`lateMedieval.statehoodFraction` (1.00)** contradicts EU4 data: 19% of provinces were still
   tribal at 1444. A value of 0.75–0.80 better reflects reality. (Though the game models "all settled
   land has nations" which is intentionally simplified.)

### `migrationFalloff` Validation

The `migrationFalloff` parameter governs how concentrated population is near settlement cradles.
Archaeological evidence strongly supports the current values:

| Era | Current Falloff | Archaeological Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `paleolithic` | 12 | ✓ Extremely concentrated near refugia and coast. Long-range dispersal rare. |
| `neolithic` | 8 | ✓ Farming spread follows river valleys; highland/arid zones empty. |
| `bronze` | 5 | ✓ States concentrated in alluvial plains; steppes/forests empty. |
| `iron` | 3 | ✓ Kingdoms expand into hinterlands; "dark zones" persist but shrink. |
| `lateMedieval` | 1.5 | ✓ Most of Eurasia settled; frontier at Atlantic, Sahara, deep Siberia. |
| `earlyModern` | 0.6 | ✓ Global colonization begins; new cradles in Americas. |
| `industrial` | 0.1 | ✓ Rail/steam erases distance penalty; settlement follows resources not proximity. |
| `information` | 0 | ✓ Population fully decoupled from geography via supply chains. |

**The `migrationFalloff` values are well-calibrated and should not change.**

## 11. Nation Size in Pre-State Eras

### Band-Level Territory (Paleolithic–Mesolithic)

"Nation size" is not meaningful for bands — they have no fixed territory and no sovereignty.
The relevant unit is the **dialect tribe** (~150 people, ~2,000–5,000 km²) which forms the
largest coherent social unit.

For a world-generation simulation with ~4,000 provinces averaging ~30,000 km² each, a single
Paleolithic dialect tribe would occupy **0.07–0.17 provinces** on average. In practice:
- Most provinces have *multiple* bands passing through seasonally
- No province is "owned" in any meaningful sense
- `hasNations: false` for `paleolithic` is architecturally correct

### Chiefdom-Level Territory (Late Neolithic–Chalcolithic)

Chiefdoms typically controlled 5,000–20,000 km², which at ~30,000 km²/province = **0.17–0.67
provinces** per chiefdom. This means:
- Most chiefdoms are smaller than one province
- A genesis "province" under a neolithic chiefdom is really a mosaic of competing chiefdoms
- `neolithic` nations of 1–2 provinces are the right scale — each "nation" represents a dominant
  chiefdom cluster

### Early State Territory (Bronze–Iron Age)

Early Bronze Age city-states: ~1,000–5,000 km² = **0.03–0.17 provinces** per city-state.
This means early states were also sub-province scale. Only empires (Akkadian, Egyptian) reached
multi-province scale.

By the Iron Age, territorial kingdoms reached 100,000–500,000 km² = **3–17 provinces** for
a typical kingdom; empires (Persia, Qin) reached **130–180 provinces** in EU4 terms.

This matches the EU4 data: **median nation size of 2 provinces at 500 AD** correctly captures
that most states were small kingdoms, while the right tail captures the Persian/Roman/Chinese
mega-empires.
